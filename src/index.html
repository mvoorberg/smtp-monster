SMTPMonster is a hosted SMTP server for development and staging. Every message your app sends is delivered to you instead, with the original From, To, CC and BCC listed on top.

Free, no credit card. Your SMTP credentials arrive at this address in about a minute.

What lands in your inbox

The real message, exactly as your app built it: subject, HTML, plain text, layout, inline images and attachments. SMTPMonster adds a block at the top so you can see who it was meant for, then sends it to the address that owns the credentials.

Because it arrives in the mail client you already use, you see it rendered the way your users would. No local mail catcher to run, no separate web UI to keep open.

How it works

Four steps, and you only do the first three once.

  1. Sign up with your email

    Enter the address you want every caught email delivered to. There's no account, no password to choose and no card.

    Enter your address above

  2. Get your SMTP credentials

    They arrive by email in about a minute. Receiving that email is the verification, and the address you signed up with is your username. This is the only time you will see this password. Store it securely.

    The credentials you receive
    Hostsmtp01.smtpmonster.com
    Port587
    Usernameyou@company.com
    Password<**********>
    SecurityTLS from the start (not STARTTLS)

  3. Point your dev or staging app at it

    Swap them into the SMTP settings your app already has. Any language or framework that sends mail over SMTP works; nothing extra to install.

    SMTP_HOST=smtp01.smtpmonster.com
SMTP_PORT=587
SMTP_SECURE=true
SMTP_USER=you@company.com
SMTP_PASS=<**********>

  4. Send email like you always do

    Password resets, receipts, digests, notifications, attachments included. Whoever they're addressed to, they come to you, with From, To, CC and BCC on top.

    • SMTPMonsterYour order has shippedto ana.lopez@gmail.com
    • SMTPMonsterReset your passwordto kim@northwind.co
    • SMTPMonsterYour weekly digestto 312 recipients

Why developers use it

We're developers too. These are the moments it was built for.

You refreshed staging from production
Your dev database is full of real customer email addresses. Run every job, trigger every workflow; none of them can email those people.
You need to see what your app sends
Not delivered to users doesn't mean invisible. Every message is in your inbox, rendered by a real mail client, ready to check before release.
You don't want another service to run
No local mail catcher, container or web UI. It's just SMTP credentials, so it works the same on your laptop, in CI and on a shared staging server.
Mistakes in dev should stay in dev
A test blast, a looping job, a misconfigured cron. With SMTPMonster in place, the worst case is a busy inbox of your own.
